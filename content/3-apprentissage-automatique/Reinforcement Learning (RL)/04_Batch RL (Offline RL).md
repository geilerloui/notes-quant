\subsection{Introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Offline RL aka Batch Reinforcement Learning, is a variant of RL that requires the agent to learn from a fixed batch of data without exploration. We will focus mostly on safe batch RL here.\\

\textbf{Example 1.} Let's say we run a scientific experiment where we have two group of people A and B. Group A starts off by solving a sequence of fractions and they reduce the sum to the lowest term. Next they do cross multiplication. Finally they pass an exam where they get an average score of 95 out of 100.\\

For Group B we repeat the same experiment but reversed they start by the cross multiplication. And they pass an exam in average they got 92.

![[images/3-Apprentissage automatique/07_Reinforcement learning/Offline/im1.png]]

Now the question is if we have a new student what should we do ? You can directly of doing the sequence or group A, but maybe the size of the sample was way smaller than group B, which would turn the estimate of the score to be biased. Basically if group B is different than group A we run into the issue of censored data. We could also think of a counterfactual reasoning what would have happened if the person was on the other group ?\\

\textbf{Example 2.} For maintenance you can search for the optimal sequence of actions that the machine do so that it runs the longest.\\


\textbf{Example 3.} For healthcare you could look at the sequence of activity you should give to a patient to maximize their outcome. One more difficulty is that it will be state dependent what works for patient A can be different on patient B. We say that this is a high stake scenario, treating people cost a lot that's why we have to find the best sequence of decisions. \\

\textbf{Problem.} From what we have just talked about we need some confidence bounds, so that the doctor how much confident he can be before deploying it.\\

\textbf{Type of safety.} (i) Safe Exploration - making sure you do not make mistake online (ii) Risk sensitivity - thinking about the fact that each of us will experience only one outcome. Which entails that the expectation won't be known to us. (iii) Here we will think mostly of being confident with respect to another policy, the one we are currently doing. We will argue that it can as we guarantee a monotonic improvement.\\

\textbf{Notation.} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Policy $\pi: \pi(a)=P\left(a_{t}=a \mid s_{t}=s\right)$
    \item Trajectory: $h=T=\left(s_{1}, a_{1}, r_{1}, s_{2}, a_{2}, r_{2}, \cdots, s_{L}, a_{L}, r_{L}\right)$
    \item Historical data: $D=\left\{T_{1}, T_{2}, \cdots, T_{n}\right\}$
    \item Historical data from behavior policy, $\pi_{b}$. Note if that wasn't given to you could learn it with Monte Carlo simulation on which you avearge the reward which let you estimate $V^{\pi_b} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{trajectory} {G_i}$
    \item Objective:
$$
V^{\pi}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{L} \gamma^{t} R_{t} \mid \pi\right]
$$
\end{itemize}

We will say that $D$ is the data we have access to: electronic medical system, data about the power plant... To simplify the problem we assume that we know what the behavior policy $\pi_b$ is. It might not be reasonable especially if the data was generated by humans. Yet for google ads for example we know exactly the steps of the algorithm so we can use their policy, or sometimes it was already an RL agent who generated the trajectories.\\

\textbf{Goal.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We have a reinforcement learning algorithm, $\mathcal{A}$
    \item Historical data, $D$, which is a random variable
    \item Policy produced by the algorithm, $\mathcal{A}(D)$, which is a random variable
    \item a safe batch reinforcement learning algorithm, $\mathcal{A}$, satisfies:
    $$
\operatorname{Pr}\left(V^{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D})} \geq V^{\pi_{b}}\right) \geq 1-\delta
$$
or in general
$$
\operatorname{Pr}\left(V^{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D})} \geq V_{\min }\right) \geq 1-\delta
$$
\end{itemize}

Basically what this is saying is that the value returned by our algorithm $V^{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D})} $ is at least as good as the behavioral policy $V^{\pi_{b}}$. And the next expression is telling you that you want the value to be not just better but substantially better by a $V_{min}$ factor. A typical situation where this could be required is that if you think of a real production system, it would cost time and money and effort when we change the protocol. So you want to change it in such a way to reduce the overhead by being significantly better.

\subsection{Model}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Off-policy policy evaluation (OPE).} For any evaluation policy, $\pi_{e}$, Convert historical data, $D$, into $n$ independent and unbiased estimates of $V^{\pi_{e}}$
    \item \textbf{High-confidence off-policy policy evaluation (HCOPE).} Use a concentration inequality to convert the $\mathrm{n}$ independent and unbiased estimates of $V^{\pi_{e}}$ into a $1-\delta$ confidence lower bound on
$V^{\pi_{e}}$
    \item \textbf{Safe policy improvement (SPI).} Use HCOPE method to create a safe batch reinforcement learning algorithm,
\end{itemize}

In the OPE setting we aim to estimate value of policy $\pi_1$ that we denote $V^{\pi_1}(s)$, given episodes generated under behavioral policy $\pi_2$
$$
D \sim \{s_{1}, a_{1}, r_{1}, s_{2}, a_{2}, r_{2}, \ldots \} 
$$
This is an off-policy setting where we want $\pi_1$ to resemble $\pi_2$. We could use Q-learning which is off-policy use its samples and bootstrap it, though, bootstrap can be biased so we will have to do in a different fashion so it is not biased. Given that we cannot have plenty of data. What we'll do next taking into account that the two policies are dissimilar is importance sampling. We also aim to make the estimator unbiased\\

\textbf{Importance Sampling.} Recall that in RL we get $h_{j}$ be episode $j$ (history) of states, actions and rewards. And by mathematical derivation we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
p\left(h_{j} \mid \pi, s=s_{j, 1}\right)=& p\left(a_{j, 1} \mid s_{j, 1}\right) p\left(r_{j, 1} \mid s_{j, 1}, a_{j, 1}\right) p\left(s_{j, 2} \mid s_{j, 1}, a_{j, 1}\right) \\
& p\left(a_{j, 2} \mid s_{j, 2}\right) p\left(r_{j, 2} \mid s_{j, 2}, a_{j, 2}\right) p\left(s_{j, 3} \mid s_{j, 2}, a_{j, 2}\right) \ldots \\
=& \prod_{t=1}^{L_{j}-1} p\left(a_{j, t} \mid s_{j, t}\right) p\left(r_{j, t} \mid s_{j, t}, a_{j, t}\right) p\left(s_{j, t+1} \mid s_{j, t}, a_{j, t}\right) \\
=& \prod_{t=1}^{L_{j}-1} \pi\left(a_{j, t} \mid s_{j, t}\right) p\left(r_{j, t} \mid s_{j, t}, a_{j, t}\right) p\left(s_{j, t+1} \mid s_{j, t}, a_{j, t}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
Next we compute the ratio:
$$
\frac{p(h_j \mid \pi_e)}{p(h_j \mid \pi_b)} = \product \frac{\pi_e(a_j \mid s_j)}{\pi_b(a_j \mid s_j)}
$$
Which in turns yields:
$$
\begin{aligned}
V^{\pi_1}(s) &\approx \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{p(h_j \mid \pi_1, s)}{p(h_j \mid \pi_2, s)} G(h_j) \\
&= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} (\prod_{i=1}^T \frac{\pi_e(a_{ij} \mid s_{ji}}{\pi_b(a_{ij} \mid s_{ji}}) G(h_j)
\end{aligned}
$$
If it was more likely under the evaluation policy we want to up-weight whatever reward we get under that trajectory and if it less likely under that evaluation policy you want to down weight it. we have $(h_1, G_1)$ pairs trajectory and sum of rewards. that's why. you want to update the $(h_1, G_1), (h_2, G_2), ...$ weight depending.  \\

Note that this is an unbiased estimator and you do not need to know the dynamics and there are no rewards and no need to be markov. W I S(D)=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_{i}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_{i}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{L} \gamma^{t} R_{t}^{i}\right)

\textbf{Theorem.}  Let $X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n}$ be $n$ independent real-valued random variables such that for each $i \in\{1, \ldots, n\}$, we have $\mathbb{P}\left[0 \leq X_{i}\right]=1, \mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right] \leq \mu$, and some threshold value $c_{i}>0 .$ Let $\delta>0$ and $Y_{i}:=\min \left\{X_{i}, c_{i}\right\} .$ Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, we have
$$
\mu \geq \underbrace{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{c_{i}}\right)^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{Y_{i}}{c_{i}}}_{\text {empirical mean }}-\underbrace{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{c_{i}}\right)^{-1} \frac{7 n \ln (2 / \delta)}{3(n-1)}}_{\text {term that goes to zero as } 1 / \text { n as } n \rightarrow \infty}-\underbrace{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{c_{i}}\right)^{-1} \sqrt{\frac{\ln (2 / \delta)}{n-1} \sum_{i, j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{Y_{i}}{c_{i}}-\frac{Y_{j}}{c_{j}}\right)^{2}}}_{\text {term that goes to zero as } 1 / \sqrt{n} \text { as } n \rightarrow \infty} .
$$
